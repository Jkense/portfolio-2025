The credit management portal for coeo started off as a design problem about complexity. The internal systems were very much legacy, and need a big UX update. At the same time, the pressure on the sales team with simple questions was on a rise.

I personally saw this as a chance given by management to show the power of design to lighten some of the burdens around the company. It's always was hard to put a number on design, but this time I think I could show some real arm strength.

That means that my own most important stakeholders are actually sales, marketing, IT (who play a key role in customer sucess) and management. The project kicked off with a round of low-hanging fruit sessions, which quickly turned into a hard set of requirements for this design sprint.

From here we prioritized:
* Establishing a design system with a clear design language
* Self-service after onboarding for delivering, handling and updating files
* One all-in-one portal for several user groups (integration engineers, accounts receivables handlers and financial management)

## The idea

That user group made for a two folded solution space, with a 

- make it easy for management to have a high over glance
- make it easy for devs to test
- make the dashboard extensible and future proof
- make the A/R employees have an easier time navigating and mass-handling files

My role sat between interface design and systems thinking. I was not just deciding how charts should look. I was deciding how users move from overview to diagnosis to action, how different layers of information should relate to each other, and how the product could stay understandable as more data and features were added over time.

## Designing the information model

<Bookmark label="Information architecture" section="design" domains="Product architecture, Information architecture, Data visualization" />

The most important design decision was to organize the portal around user intent rather than around data sources or internal system structure. We wanted navigation to solve the pains that sales and IT was hearing during their customer calls

Instead of relying on irregular shaped ux, I prioritized a typical dashbaord, that helped destructure and simplify ux across the board.

Iconography, color choice, and common ux patterns such as modals, table design were all brought into the design system as a conscuious choice, to make the navigation as simple as possible.

This is not only important for the current flow of the product, but for the strategic need that was stated by management in the long run too. 

That gave the product a clearer shape.

## From dashboards to decision-making

A core principle throughout the project was that the interface should prioritize insight over display. I did not want the main dashboard to feel like a collection of charts. It needed to function as a high over view for management, IT and A/R to understand their case loads.

That meant that I included a less minimal design phase during the sprints, which were eventually left out due to resource limitations from the IT team, taht wcould be possible ays to extend the product long term. 

This included a few more complicated graphs, heavily inspired by common analytical software, and would allow the same teams to get interesting insights that would otherwise be difficult to spot, such as a geographical case spread and performance, a drop in behaviour in, for example, text message traffic that could directly signify the IT team and emails for a high over view for management.

## Technical thinking behind the design

The design not only included important UX decisions, but also simple to implement design system challenges. That means that I, as a designer, could influence how we build with the dev team to speed up our workflow
